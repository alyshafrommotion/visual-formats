# Greenscreen Format — Execution SOP

For "is this ad a Greenscreen?" naming/classification questions, use `classification.md` in this same folder instead. This document is for building and scripting one.

## Reference examples (look here first)

- **Reference examples:** They sit in this same folder, right next to this SOP.
- This SOP lives in this folder, so the example videos sit next to it.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, open this folder and watch the examples first** — what they put on screen behind the creator, how fast the screen changes, and the reaction energy.

## What this format is

A creator stands (usually cut-out) in front of a full-frame piece of "evidence" — a screenshot, a headline, a product page, a review, a chart, a competitor's site — and reacts to it in real time while it stays on screen. It works because the evidence is doing the persuading and the creator is doing the pointing; it feels like a friend showing you their phone, not an ad reading copy.

Strongest for **proof, myth-busting, comparisons, "look what I found," reframing an ingredient/claim, and reacting to social/press.** The screen is the star; the creator is the guide.

The one property to protect: **the on-screen evidence must be genuinely interesting on its own,** and the creator must *react* to it, not narrate it.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the point to prove), persona and awareness stage, the **evidence assets** (real screenshots, reviews, studies, pages, comments — sourced, not faked), and claim facts. The angle picks the point; the evidence proves it; this SOP governs how the screen and the reaction are built.

If there's no real evidence to show, get it. A greenscreen with a fabricated screenshot is a liability, not a format.

## Anatomy — the layers

### 1 · The evidence (background, the star)
- Full-frame behind the creator: screenshot, article, page, chart, comment, DM, label.
- It must read in ~1–2 seconds and be legible at thumb size (zoom/crop to the one line that matters; highlight or circle it live).
- One piece of evidence per beat. When the point changes, the screen changes.

### 2 · The creator (guide)
- Cut-out or seated in front of the evidence. Real, casual, mid-life energy — not studio.
- Gestures at the screen: points, circles, zooms. The body sells "look at this."

### 3 · The reaction script (the format)
- The creator reacts first, explains second. "Wait — look at this." then the point.
- Beats move fast; each new claim = new evidence on screen.

## Script / build rules (priority order)

1. **React, don't narrate.** Open on the reaction ("okay this is wild," "no because look"), then the evidence, then the point. A flat read-through of the screen kills it.
2. **The screen carries the proof; the voice carries the meaning.** Don't say what's already legible on screen — say what it *means*.
3. **One evidence, one point, then cut.** New claim, new screenshot. Momentum is the format.
4. **Point at it.** Circle, highlight, zoom the exact line. If the viewer has to hunt for what you mean, you lost them.
5. **Casual, low-effort look is a feature, not a bug** — it's what makes the evidence feel found. (This is the casual-disarm / effort-mismatch mechanic: the production reads as found, not made.)
6. **Specificity is credibility.** Real sources, real numbers, real quotes on screen. Never fabricate a screenshot or a stat. No absolutes.
7. **Brand/product enters as the payoff** of the evidence, once, near the end — "this is why I switched to [brand]."
8. **Banned:** reading the screen verbatim, fake screenshots, "trust me," "designed to," "seamless," absolutes, and a static screen for more than one point.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Hook (first reaction) | ≤3s, ≤10 words |
| Beats | 3–6, each with its own on-screen evidence |
| Total runtime | 15–40s |
| Spoken lines | short, ≤15 words |
| Brand mention | 1, as the payoff |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage.
2. Gather the real evidence assets; verify each is legit and legible.
3. Sequence 3–6 evidence beats that build to the point.
4. Write the hook reaction, then one reaction line per evidence beat (meaning, not read-through).
5. Mark the exact highlight/zoom/circle for each screen.
6. Place the brand as the payoff of the last beat.
7. Claims pass: every on-screen stat/quote sourced and validated.
8. Production handoff: legible full-frame evidence, creator cut-out with gestures, fast cuts synced to screen changes, captions on.
9. Deliver: evidence sequence with highlights, VO reaction script by beat, brand payoff line, sources/claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Every screen is real, legible in 1–2s, and highlighted
- [ ] Creator reacts first, explains the meaning, doesn't read the screen
- [ ] One evidence per point; screen changes with each beat
- [ ] Hook reaction lands in ≤3s
- [ ] Brand enters once, as the payoff
- [ ] Every on-screen stat/quote sourced; no fabricated evidence; no absolutes
- [ ] Reads/plays legibly at thumb size
