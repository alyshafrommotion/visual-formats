# High-Craft Edit — Classification Guide

## Definition

A **video** whose defining quality is the **craft and density of the edit itself**. A voiceover drives a fast montage, and every beat gets its own purpose-built visual payoff: a dramatized demo (phone dropped from the sky, crane-drop, water test), a built animation (exploded-view diagram, motion graphic), a meme or reaction insert, an object-in-motion gag. No shot is held long; the edit keeps topping itself so a wall-to-wall feature or claim rundown stays genuinely fun to watch. The **entertainment is the editing**, not a single talking head or a static layout.

High-Craft Edit is the **anchor (primary)** format. It can pair with a secondary via the dual-format rule (e.g. + demo when a single sustained product demo also carries a chunk of the ad, or + listicle when the payoffs are enumerated).

## The things that must all be true

1. **It's a video.** Never a static.
2. **A voiceover (or fast on-screen text) drives the pace** and the visuals are cut tightly to it.
3. **Almost every beat has its own built visual payoff** — a staged gag, effect, animation, insert, or dramatized demo — not just a plain product shot or a person talking.
4. **The edit is dense and keeps escalating:** shots swap constantly, payoffs vary in kind, and the craft is high enough that watching the edit is itself the reward.
5. **The production is deliberately built**, not incidental b-roll: the gags and animations were made for this ad.

## What the edit does

The point of the format is that the editing carries the entertainment so a heavy, feature-packed script never gets boring:

- **Dramatize a claim:** stage a literal stunt for it (drop test, crane-drop, hang-from-a-cable, water splash).
- **Build the invisible:** exploded-view animations and motion graphics show internal parts or mechanisms the camera can't.
- **Punch a beat:** a meme, reaction, or hard smash-cut insert lands a joke or emphasis.
- **Keep escalating:** each payoff tries to top the last, so momentum never drops even across a long rundown.

## Quick test

Mute it, then ask: is the reason this is fun to watch the **editing** — a relentless stack of built gags, stunts, and animations cut to the VO, each topping the last? If the craft of the edit is doing the entertaining (not a single held anchor shot, not a plain demo, not a person just talking), it's High-Craft Edit.

## Commonly confused with

- **Graphic-anchor:** a creator holds the frame as the anchor while graphics accent them over the same shot. High-Craft Edit cuts away constantly and the built payoffs *are* the ad; there's no held anchor. If graphics merely float around a sustained talking creator, it's graphic-anchor.
- **Yapper:** a fast talking-head monologue with no on-screen evidence. High-Craft Edit is wall-to-wall built evidence, stunts, and animation. A VO can narrate, but the visuals are the show.
- **Demo:** one product being used/shown, sustained, to prove it works. High-Craft Edit may contain demo beats but strings many dramatized payoffs together; when a single sustained demo carries the ad, tag demo (or dual-tag).
- **Asmr:** sound-led, satisfying tactile product sounds with little VO. High-Craft Edit is VO-and-visual-led and gag-dense, not sound-led.

## Supporting signals (not required)

- Built animations (exploded views, motion graphics) mixed with live stunts
- Staged stunts or stress tests standing in for durability/quality claims
- Meme or reaction inserts used as punchlines
- Sound-design punches (whooshes, impacts, clicks) synced to cuts
- A feature/claim count that would be boring shot flat, kept alive purely by the edit
