# Comment Response Format — Implementation SOP

## What this format is

At its core: a question posed through an Instagram or TikTok comment-response sticker (the question-sticker mechanic), acknowledged and answered by a real-seeming person, built so nothing in it reads as an ad. The viewer isn't being addressed. They are reading (or watching) someone else's exchange. That is why the format works, and it only works while it stays native. The one property to protect above everything: **the reply must read like a real person, typed or spoken.** One corporate sentence kills the whole unit.

**Where the response sticker comes from (pick one):**
- Instagram question / comment-response sticker (the black-bar prompt plus white question box). The original AMA form.
- The TikTok equivalent comment-response sticker.

A screenshotted real comment is a *different* format (see the comment-screenshot / `instagram-comment` format). This format is always the question / comment-response sticker, never a plain comment bubble.

**How the reply is delivered (pick one):**
- **Static / text-overlay:** free-placed native text answering the sticker question over a selfie, POV, or lifestyle image. This is the detailed playbook below (Anatomy, Register rules, Length caps).
- **Video:** the creator reads or references the sticker question on camera and their spoken response is the ad. Same register and voice rules apply to the VO. See the "Video variant" section below for what changes.

The rest of this SOP is written for the static / text-overlay build, the most exacting version. Every voice and register rule carries straight over to the video build; the "Video variant" section covers only what is different.

## Inputs

This SOP executes; it doesn't strategize. Expect to be given a **messaging angle** (what the ad argues), plus persona and moment context, and whatever VOC assets the project holds (call transcripts, review audits, swipe files, comment scrapes). The angle decides what is said; this SOP governs how.

If no VOC is available, say so and gather language first — the question and reply are built from real speech, not invented speech.

## Anatomy — four layers

### 1 · Sticker bar (black pill)

Two to five words naming the ritual, not the brand. One emoji max. "Ask me anything" is the reliable default; a custom sticker may name the audience or the scene instead. Never "FAQ."

On TikTok, use the platform's equivalent comment-response sticker rather than IG's black pill; the mechanic is identical (a short prompt the reply answers). Match the exact styling to the examples in this folder.

**Match the sticker to the concept when one fits.** The default is "Ask me anything," but in a perfect world the sticker is a short prompt that ties to what the ad is actually about, e.g. "questions about creative strategy?" for a creative-strategy concept, or "ama about ai" for an AI concept. Concept-matched beats generic; fall back to "Ask me anything" only when nothing natural fits.

Observed live variants: the abbreviated **"AMA"**, and "Ask me anything" softened with one persona emoji ("Ask me anything 💕", "Ask me anything 😊"). Keep it to one emoji and keep it in the persona's world, not the brand's.

The load-bearing choice is **me vs us**: "me" means a person answers and every register rule below applies at full strength; "us" (e.g. "Ask us anything!") means the brand answers, which unlocks specs and a CTA (see the brand-cast exception). The sticker pronoun is what signals which mode the viewer is in, so pick it deliberately.

### 2 · Question (white box)

One sentence, 7–15 words (sweet spot 8–12), first person, one emoji max. Usually ends in "?", but a short plea reads just as native ("Help", "what do I do") when the moment is desperate enough.

- The question does the targeting: written so precisely in the persona's own words that the wrong person scrolls past and the right person feels seen.
- Typed register, not written register — contractions, casual phrasing, self-reported emotion, the way people actually type into a question sticker.
- Build it from VOC: find a real quote near the messaging angle and compress it to the cap. If the question contains no phrase a real person actually said, rewrite it.
- **Bake the objection into the question when there is one.** The strongest questions carry the exact gatekeeping or myth the reply will overturn, so the reframe has something to push against (e.g. Winona: "My doctor told me I'm 'too young' for HRT and tried to put me on birth control instead. Help" → the reply dismantles the "too young" gatekeep). The question states the wall; the reply knocks it down.
- Whether the brand is named in the question is a judgment call driven by the brief's awareness stage: naming it presumes the viewer already knows the product; keeping the question problem-only doesn't. Decide from the brief, not by default.
- **Proven question openers (from the folder), by intent:** the volunteer prompt ("What's the number one thing you'd repurchase?") lets the answerer surface the product unprompted; the switch question ("Why did you switch to [brand]?") sets up a ❌/✅ split; the functional objection ("Can you still hear people if you wear them in a social setting?") sets up a verdict-first answer; the self-blame pain question ("Why does every lipstick settle into the lines in my lips?") sets up an it's-not-you reframe; the gatekeep/frustration question ("Why do I feel super scammed trying to start [X]?") sets up validation + mechanism.

### 3 · Image and casting

Selfie, mirror selfie, POV product-in-hand, or lifestyle shot. The person is **mid-life, not mid-ad** — no studio, ring light, or podcast mic. Match the setting to the persona's actual day.

**Peer answers peer:** the answerer must be castable as someone the asker would really ask. If the viewer wouldn't put this question to this person, the ad is miscast regardless of copy.

Product in frame optional. If present, held like an object, not presented like a prize.

### 4 · Reply (native text overlay)

Free-placed blocks around the subject. The blocks are the paragraphs — the visual gaps do the pacing, so never one wall of text. Choose ONE architecture per ad, whichever fits the question type:

- **Verdict-first story** — the answer lands in the first line, the story follows. Default for direct questions.
- **Validation opener** — the first line mirrors the asker's emotion before anything else. For emotionally loaded questions.
- **❌/✅ split** — up to three before-bullets, three after-bullets. For switching and comparison questions.
- **It's-not-you reframe** — absolve the asker, blame the old way, introduce the new one. For questions where the asker blames themselves.
- **Spec-stack in human voice** — specs allowed only when wrapped in feeling or outcome; the sentence ends on what it meant, never on the spec.
- **Concede-first** — for skeptic questions: agree with the premise before the story turns.

**The three-beat close (most common in the folder):** reframe or verdict → mechanism (why this one is different) → dated outcome. Winona runs it cleanly: "Doctors get less than 20% training on menopause…" (reframe) → "I use Winona — they prescribe based on symptoms, not age or one-off lab tests" (mechanism) → "Within 3 weeks my mood swings were gone and I was sleeping through the night again" (dated outcome). The dated outcome ("within 3 weeks", "by day 4") is what makes the payoff feel earned instead of claimed.

**Trust markers, in human voice.** In credential-heavy or regulated categories, one real badge or certification dropped mid-reply buys trust — but only phrased the way a person would ("…LegitScript certified 👍", "it's greenguard gold certified (nontoxic and super safe!)"). The badge must be real and the brand must actually hold it. Never invent a certification to sound legit.

**Styling levers (from the examples).** The reply overlay's font and highlight-box color are a brand lever, not a default:
- **Bold sans in highlight boxes** reads casual and native (Hims, Loop). **Bold italic** adds energy (Preply). **Elegant serif** reads considered and premium — right for health/beauty/wellness (Winona, Thrive).
- **Tint the highlight boxes with the brand's accent color** to tie the ad in without breaking the native feel (Preply's yellow). Keep contrast high and the text legible at thumb size.
- Whatever the treatment, the words still have to read like a person typed them. Styling makes it on-brand; it never makes processed copy pass.

## Video variant (on-camera response)

Use this when the reply is spoken or enacted instead of typed (e.g. the Javvy example in this folder). Everything about voice, verdict-first structure, and native feel is identical; only the delivery changes.

- **Show the sticker, then answer it.** Open by surfacing the question / comment-response sticker (the IG question sticker or the TikTok equivalent), then respond. The viewer must see or hear what is being answered within the first 2 seconds.
- **The hook is the sticker question plus the first line of the answer.** Let the question sit on screen (or read it) and go straight into the verdict or the reaction. No "hey guys," no intro. Same rule as the static first line: react to the person or land the verdict, never context-set.
- **Reference the question out loud.** "Someone asked…", "you asked…", "replying to this…" makes the response feel earned. The question is the reason the video exists, so name it.
- **Casting and setting:** peer answers peer, mid-life not mid-ad. Front-camera selfie, talking head, or POV. No studio polish. The same "would the asker really ask this person" test applies.
- **Keep text overlay as reinforcement, not a script.** A short on-screen caption of the question (and optionally a one-line verdict) helps sound-off viewers, but the spoken answer carries it. Do not paste the full static reply on screen and read it word for word.
- **Structure the VO on the same architectures** (verdict-first story, validation opener, it's-not-you reframe, concede-first). The three-beat close still lands best: reframe or verdict, then mechanism (why this one is different), then a dated outcome ("within 3 weeks…").
- **Length:** treat it like a short talking-head. Aim 15 to 40 seconds. The answer starts immediately and the product enters mid-story, named once the way a person would say it. Captions on.
- **Claims travel with the format.** A spoken anecdote-outcome in a regulated category is still a claim and still needs substantiation; any certification named must be real and current.

## Register rules (priority order)

1. **Answer the person, not the topic.** The first line reacts to the asker or delivers the verdict — never context-setting, never a thesis.
2. **Verdict first, reasons after.** Real people lead with the answer and then explain. Corporate copy builds to the answer. Flip it.
3. **Story beats advice.** Past tense, lived, on the spine *been there → what I did → what changed*. Advice is only allowed when the question literally asks "how do I." Everything else gets a story.
4. **One mundane concrete detail and one admitted feeling in every reply.** The detail makes it true; the feeling makes it a person.
5. **Imperfection is credibility.** The answerer admits something — a thing they got wrong, a habit they haven't fixed. A flawless answer reads scripted, because it was.
6. **Speech artifacts are the seasoning — 2 to 4 per ad, never zero, never all:** sentence fragments; CAPS on one spoken-stress word; sentences starting with And/But/So/Instead; shrug phrases; enthusiasm doubling; parenthetical asides. One emoji per block, max.
7. **The brand is named once, mid-story, the way a person would say it in conversation.** An @tag counts. Two mentions max on product-aware retargets. Never a positioning phrase attached to the name.
8. **The brand-cast exception:** only when the sticker says "us" may the reply carry specs, named products, and a closing CTA — and even then the first line must be human.
9. **Banned:** "great question," "we're glad you asked," "we understand," moreover/additionally/furthermore, semicolons, ™/® in creator-cast, "designed to," "solutions," "seamless," and absolutes like "works with everything" — name real things; specificity is the credibility and skeptics screenshot absolutes.
10. **Dictate before typing.** Say the answer out loud as if a friend asked, transcribe it, trim. Then read every block aloud and rewrite anything you stumble on. This step is mandatory, not polish.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Sticker bar | 2–5 words, ≤1 emoji |
| Question | 1 sentence, 7–15 words AND ≤90 characters, whichever is shorter, ≤2 lines in the box. Past ~90 chars it gets cut off in-feed (a 108-char question cut off mid-sentence). |
| Reply blocks | 2–5 per ad; each 1–2 sentences, ≤25 words (~150 characters) |
| The one long block | ONE block per ad may reach ~30 words (~180 characters) — only if it reads as a single breath |
| Sentences | ≤15 words (~90 characters) otherwise |
| Bullets | 2–8 words each, ≤3 per side, ≤6 total |
| Reply total | consumer: 25–45 words · B2B/considered: 45–75 · **hard ceiling: 80 words (~430 characters)** |
| Whole ad | ≤95 words including sticker and question |

If the idea needs more than 80 words, it's two ads. Cut order when over: the final summary block first (it's always the most brand-flavored and the ad already ended without it) → qualifiers and second adjectives → never fix length by making sentences denser. Dense is processed; short is human.

**Production gauge:** if the designer has to shrink the type to fit, the copy is too long. Type size is the length check that can't be argued with.

## Build steps

1. **Read the brief:** messaging angle, persona, moment, awareness stage.
2. **Pull 5–10 verbatim VOC lines** near the angle; mark two phrases to reuse.
3. **Write the question as the asker** — a real quote compressed to the cap, emotion kept in.
4. **Choose architecture and casting** — one architecture; me or us; who answers; what they're mid-doing in the image.
5. **Dictate the reply, transcribe, trim** to the caps. Verdict or validation goes first.
6. **Register pass** — rules 1–10, in order.
7. **Length pass** — caps and cut order.
8. **Claims pass** — no absolutes; every number and capability validated against the project's claim flags. **In regulated categories (Rx/health, hormones, supplements, baby safety, finance), a personal-anecdote outcome ("within 3 weeks my mood swings were gone") is still a claim** — it needs substantiation and claim-flag review, and any certification named (LegitScript, greenguard, clinical) must be real and current. The story voice does not exempt the copy from compliance.
9. **Design handoff** — question box in the top third (it's the hook and gets read first); blocks placed with visible gaps; alternating alignment fine; CTA pill only on brand-cast or warm retarget. For video: the response sticker anchored in the first beat, captions on.
10. **Deliver:** sticker text, question, reply blocks in order (or VO script for video), casting note, image/shot direction, any claims flags.

## QA checklist

- [ ] Question ≤15 words and reads typed by a thumb, not written by a strategist
- [ ] First reply line is a verdict or a reaction to the person — not context-setting
- [ ] Past-tense story spine present (unless the question asked "how do I")
- [ ] One mundane concrete detail and one admitted feeling
- [ ] Brand named once, mid-story (twice max on product-aware)
- [ ] All length caps respected
- [ ] 2–4 speech artifacts; ≤1 emoji per block
- [ ] Every block passes the read-aloud test
- [ ] No banned phrases, no absolutes, all claims validated
- [ ] Regulated category checked: anecdote-as-claim substantiated, any named certification real and current
- [ ] Sticker pronoun (me/us) matches the mode; any trust badge named is real
- [ ] Screenshot test: dropped into a stories feed with no context, nothing reads as an ad before the brand mention
- [ ] (Video) The specific sticker question is shown or named in the first 2 seconds, and the answer starts immediately
- [ ] (Video) Spoken reply passes the same register rules; captions on; product enters mid-story, named once
- [ ] (Platform) The source is a question / comment-response sticker (Instagram's or TikTok's), not a screenshotted comment
