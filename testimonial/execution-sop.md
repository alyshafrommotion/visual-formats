# Testimonial Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

They sit in this same folder, next to this SOP. Open the folder and study the example first: how the arc (struggle, turning point, result) is paced and where the product enters.

## What this format is

One real person narrating their own experience start to finish: where they were, the moment the product changed things, and where they are now. It works because a lived, first-person arc is more believable and more emotional than a claim. Almost always video.

Strongest for **considered, trust-driven purchases** and **audiences who need to see someone like them get the result.**

The one property to protect: **it must be a real, full story.** Strip the arc down to one line and it becomes a Review, not a Testimonial.

## Review vs Testimonial

Build a Testimonial when you want the whole narrated journey (usually video), someone telling their before-to-after in their own words. Build a Review when you want a fast, single pulled-quote hit (static-leaning), read in one glance. Same raw material (a happy customer), different format: Testimonial is the narrated arc; Review is the one-glance quote card.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle**, a **real customer** willing to tell their story, and the **true arc** (their struggle, the turning point, the result). The angle picks which beat to open on.

## Anatomy

1. **The struggle / before:** the relatable problem in their words.
2. **The turning point:** discovering and using the product.
3. **The result / after:** the concrete change.
4. **Product context** woven through, not bolted on.
5. **Brand + CTA**, with disclaimers where results are claimed.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Keep the full arc.** Before, turning point, after. If it collapses to one line, it's a Review.
2. **Real person, real words.** Never script it into something they wouldn't say; never fabricate.
3. **Open on the strongest beat** the angle calls for (often the relatable struggle or the surprising result).
4. **Land a specific result**, not a vague rave.
5. **Product enters naturally** as the turning point.
6. **One CTA. No absolutes / banned phrases.** Add "results may vary" where a result is claimed.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Struggle/before | keep tight, 1 beat |
| Turning point | 1 beat |
| Result/after | 1 beat, specific |
| CTA | 1 |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, the customer, their true arc.
2. Structure the three beats: struggle, turning point, result.
3. Choose the opening beat per the angle.
4. Weave in product context and one concrete result.
5. Add brand and one CTA; add "results may vary" where needed.
6. Claims pass (story real; result claims disclaimed; no absolutes/banned phrases).
7. Deliver: the three-beat arc, product context, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] A real person telling a full before/turning-point/after arc (not a one-line Review)
- [ ] Real words, real story, specific result
- [ ] Product enters as the turning point; one CTA
- [ ] "Results may vary" where a result is claimed; no absolutes/banned phrases
