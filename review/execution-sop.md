# Review Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

They sit in this same folder, next to this SOP. Open the folder and study the examples first: both the single pulled-quote version and the multi-card grid, and how quote(s), star ratings, attribution, and product work together at a glance.

## What this format is

A creative built around real customer reviews: a single short quote and/or star rating, or several arranged together, each attributed, sitting on or beside the product. It works because it is fast, credible, and skimmable in seconds. It is the quick-hit cousin of the Testimonial. One review or many, it stays a Review.

Strongest for **scroll-stopping social proof at the top of funnel** and **products where sharp customer lines land the benefit.**

The one property to protect: **it must stay quick and skimmable.** The moment it becomes one person's narrated journey, it is a Testimonial, not a Review.

## Review vs Testimonial

Build a Review when you want fast review snippets, one pulled quote or a clean grid of several (static-leaning). Build a Testimonial when you want a person to tell their whole story on camera, start to finish. Same raw material (happy customers), different format: Review is the one-glance quote card(s); Testimonial is the narrated arc.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle**, one or more **real customer quotes** (verbatim), their **star ratings**, the **attributions** (name or initial), and a product shot.

## Anatomy

1. **The review(s):** one short, specific customer line, or several arranged in a clean grid, in quotation marks.
2. **Star rating(s):** the visual credibility cue.
3. **Attribution(s):** real names or initials ("- Krista R.").
4. **Product:** in frame, behind or beside the review(s).
5. **Brand + CTA**, plus a "results may vary" footnote where claims require it.

## Build rules (priority order)

1. **Keep it quick and skimmable.** One review or a clean grid of several, but no narrated arc. If it becomes one person's journey, it's a Testimonial.
2. **Use real, verbatim quotes** with real attributions. Never fabricate reviews.
3. **Lead with the sharpest, most specific lines** the angle calls for (a concrete result beats a vague rave).
4. **Product stays in frame** so the proof attaches to the thing being sold.
5. **Legible at thumb size** (watch density on multi-card grids).
6. **One CTA. No absolutes / banned phrases.** Add "results may vary" where a result is claimed.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Each quote | ≤ 20 words |
| Each attribution | 1 line |
| CTA | 1 |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, the chosen review(s), ratings, attributions.
2. Set the review(s) as the hero and add star ratings.
3. Add attributions and the product shot.
4. Add brand and one CTA; add the "results may vary" footnote if a result is claimed.
5. Claims pass (quotes real and verbatim; result claims disclaimed).
6. Legibility check at thumb size.
7. Deliver: review(s), ratings, attributions, product, CTA, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Customer review(s), one or several, quick and skimmable (not a narrated story)
- [ ] Quotes are real and verbatim with real attributions
- [ ] Product in frame; one CTA
- [ ] "Results may vary" where a result is claimed; no absolutes/banned phrases
