# Us Vs Them — Classification Guide

## Definition

A **two-column comparison**: the brand on one side (green checks), an alternative (named competitor, category, or "the old way") on the other (red X's), across 4-7 rows of buyer-language criteria.

## The three things that must all be true

1. **The comparison is the creative's main focus** — the side-by-side is the thing the viewer is meant to read, not a competitor that is merely implied, name-checked, or sitting in frame.
2. **There are two clearly labeled columns** (us vs. them/old way), not a single-sided list of benefits.
3. **Each row is a check/X (or equivalent binary) comparison** across a shared criterion, not annotated call-outs on one product.

## Quick test

Ask: *is a two-column, brand-wins-each-row comparison against an alternative the main event of this creative?* If yes, Us Vs Them. If the versus is only implied or incidental, it is not.

## What does NOT count (implied is not Us Vs Them)

A competitor being *implied* is not Us Vs Them. None of these qualify on their own:

- A rival product simply sitting in the frame (e.g. a competitor tub in a lifestyle or flatlay shot).
- Name-checking a competitor or "the other greens" in copy without a structured side-by-side.
- A one-sided benefit or checklist that only beats an unnamed "them" by implication.

When the comparison is not the main focus, tag the format the ad actually is (e.g. comment-response, feature-benefit-pointout) and let a switching/comparison **angle** carry the versus energy. The angle is the argument; the format is how it looks.

## Commonly confused with

- **Feature Benefit Pointout:** also has multiple labeled points, but they're call-outs on a single product (feature → benefit), not a two-column brand-vs-alternative table.
- **Comment-response (and other single-product formats) with a competitor in frame:** a reply or lifestyle creative that name-checks or shows a rival (e.g. "I reviewed 3 popular greens" with a competitor tub visible) is still that format, not Us Vs Them. There is no side-by-side table doing the work.

## What to look for as supporting signals

- A framing headline that names the comparison's criteria
- 4-7 rows, strongest contrast first
- Either a named competitor (higher scrutiny, needs claim clearance) or "the old way"/category (safer default)
