# EGC (Employee-Generated Content) Format — Execution SOP

For "is this ad an EGC?" naming/classification questions, use `classification.md` in this same folder instead. This document is for building one.

## Reference examples

No example creatives yet. Add real in-market EGC examples via the intake SOP, then calibrate this SOP against them (and get a founder-style deep read on how to execute it well).

## What this format is

An employee of the brand, on camera, speaking on behalf of the brand from the inside. The credibility comes from "I actually work here": staff picks, behind-the-scenes, what the team really thinks, what customers get wrong. It sits between a founder ad (the originator, in the weeds on product development) and a creator/testimonial (an outsider). The employee has insider access and can represent the brand, but stays more high-level than a founder.

Strongest for **trust and relatability, behind-the-scenes, staff recommendations, "how it's really made/handled," and humanizing the brand.**

The one property to protect: **it has to read as a real employee talking, insider but honest** — not a founder claiming authorship, and not a scripted spokesperson.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle**, persona and awareness stage, the **employee's real vantage** (their role, what they see day to day, what they'd recommend), and claim facts. The angle picks the point; the employee's insider view makes it credible.

## Anatomy — the beats

- **Insider hook:** open on the employee framing ("I work at [brand], and…") plus the one insider line that stops the scroll. Not "Hi, I'm an employee" as filler, the hook is the insider reveal or recommendation.
- **The insider point:** what they see, recommend, or want to clear up, from the inside. High-level, not founder-deep.
- **The support:** one concrete, real detail from working there.
- **The ask:** one plain next step.

## Script / voice rules (priority order)

1. **Employee voice, on behalf of the brand.** "Here at [brand]," "our team," "I work here." Insider, but not the originator.
2. **Stay higher-level than a founder.** Recommendations, behind-the-scenes, what people get wrong, not the origin story or deep product-development detail (that's Founder).
3. **One real insider detail** that only someone who works there would offer.
4. **Open on the insider hook, not an intro.**
5. **Specificity is credibility.** Real, honest, no absolutes.
6. **Brand named naturally,** as their employer, not as a positioning line.
7. **Banned:** "I started/created this" (that's Founder), "we're on a mission to," "game-changer," "designed to," "seamless," and any line a non-employee could say identically.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Hook | 1 line, ≤12 words, first 3s |
| Total runtime | 15–40s |
| Spoken lines | short, ≤15 words |
| Brand mentions | 1–2, natural |
| The ask | 1 sentence, 1 action |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage.
2. Pull the employee's real vantage and one insider detail.
3. Write the insider hook first.
4. Script the insider point → support → ask; keep it higher-level than a founder.
5. Dictate-transcribe-trim; read-aloud pass.
6. Register + claims pass (rules 1–7; validate every number).
7. Deliver: hook line, VO script by section, setting/casting note (employee, workplace optional), on-screen text, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Reads as a real employee of the brand, not the founder and not an outsider
- [ ] Stays higher-level than a founder (no origin/product-development depth, no "I created this")
- [ ] One real insider detail
- [ ] Insider hook lands in the first 3s
- [ ] Brand named naturally as their employer
- [ ] Every claim validated; no absolutes; no banned lines
