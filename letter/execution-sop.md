# Letter Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

- **Reference examples:** They sit in this same folder, right next to this SOP.
- This SOP lives in this folder, so the example creatives sit next to it.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, open this folder and study the examples first** — the headline hook, letter length, how the text block is set, whether and how it's signed, and how personal vs promotional they read.

## What this format is

An **image** ad (never a video) whose main event is a block of earnest, first-person written copy: **one title headline that acts as the hook**, then a chunky paragraph or a couple of paragraphs that argue or confess. It works because a letter signals sincerity and singular address — one person writing to one reader — and because long copy that earns attention converts a considered buyer better than a slogan.

Very often it's an **apology** or trust-repair note, and very often it's **signed by a founder, CEO, or someone of status** at the business — but neither is required. Sometimes there's imagery, but the **text is always the primary focus.**

Strongest for **mission/values, apology or trust-repair, price/quality justification, a big change or relaunch, and warm/retargeting audiences** who will read. Weak for pure impulse promo.

The one property to protect: **it must read like a person wrote it to one reader,** not like a brand broadcasting. The headline and the signature do most of the work.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the argument or admission), persona and awareness stage, the real **brand/founder context or customer story**, and claim facts. The angle sets the point; the brand context supplies the truth and the voice; this SOP governs structure and register.

If there's no genuine reason for a letter (a real belief, change, apology, or story), don't force it — a letter with nothing to say reads as costume.

## Anatomy — the layers

### 1 · The title headline (the hook)
- One line at the top that earns the read of the block below. Often the apology, the confession, the promise, or the belief stated plainly. This is what stops the scroll — the "hook" for the whole letter.

### 2 · Salutation (optional)
- If used: direct, human, singular. "Hey," "To the person who's tried everything," "Dear over-thinker." Names the reader or the moment, not the segment. Not required.

### 3 · The body (the text block — 1–3 chunky paragraphs)
- The primary focus of the ad. One idea per paragraph, on a clear spine: **problem/belief/apology → what we did → what it means for you.**
- Chunky but readable: short paragraphs with visible gaps — the whitespace paces the read. Never one uninterrupted wall.
- Product enters as the resolution, not the subject.

### 4 · The signature (very common, not required)
- Very often a real name from the **founder, CEO, or someone of status** at the business, with a role, and sometimes a P.S. that carries the offer or the single strongest line. Handwritten-signature styling optional. A letter can also go unsigned.

### 5 · Imagery (optional, secondary)
- Sometimes there's a photo or light imagery, but the text block is always the primary focus. Keep imagery quiet so the words lead.

## Register rules (priority order)

1. **It's an image, and the text block is the ad.** Never a video; keep any imagery secondary to the words.
2. **Write to one person.** Second-person singular, warm, direct. "You," not "our customers."
3. **The headline is the hook.** Open on the sharpest thing — the apology, confession, belief, or promise — so the block below gets read. No throat-clearing.
4. **One idea per paragraph; short paragraphs.** Gaps do the pacing.
5. **Story and belief beat features.** Lead with the lived thing or the conviction; product is the resolution.
6. **One mundane concrete detail and one admitted feeling.** Specificity + humanity are what make a letter land.
7. **Say the belief plainly.** A letter is allowed conviction and vulnerability a banner isn't. Use them.
8. **Specificity is credibility.** Real numbers, real decisions. No absolutes.
9. **Brand named sparingly, mid-letter,** the way a person refers to their own thing. The P.S. may carry one clear ask.
10. **Sign it like a person** when signed — a real name and role (often the founder/CEO). If unsigned, the voice still has to feel singular.
11. **Read aloud.** A letter has to sound like a voice. Rewrite anything you stumble on. Cut every sentence that doesn't advance the argument or the feeling.
12. **Banned:** "we're excited to announce," "we're on a mission to," corporate connectors (moreover/furthermore), "designed to," "seamless," "solutions," and any paragraph doing two jobs.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Title headline (hook) | 1 line, ≤12 words |
| Salutation (if used) | ≤6 words |
| Body | 1–3 chunky paragraphs, each 1–4 sentences, sentences ≤20 words |
| Whole letter | consumer 70–140 words · considered/high-AOV up to ~200 |
| Signature | name + role; P.S. optional, 1 sentence |

Cut order when over: the summary paragraph first (the letter already ended), then qualifiers and second adjectives. Never fix length by densifying — dense reads corporate; short reads human.

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage; confirm there's a real reason for a letter.
2. Pull the true story/belief and the one concrete detail from brand context.
3. Choose the spine (problem/apology → what we did → what it means).
4. Write the title headline first; make it the scroll-stopper.
5. Draft the body, one idea per paragraph; product as resolution.
6. Add the signature (name/role, often founder/CEO) and, if it helps, a P.S. that carries the ask or best line.
7. Read-aloud + register pass (rules 1–12).
8. Length pass and claims pass (validate every number).
9. Design handoff: **static image** styled as a letter/note, prominent headline, generous paragraph gaps, signature treatment, quiet or no product imagery so the words lead.
10. Deliver: headline, salutation (if any), body paragraphs in order, signature, P.S., styling note, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] It's an image, never a video; the text block is the primary focus (imagery, if any, is secondary)
- [ ] One title headline acts as the hook and earns the read
- [ ] Reads like one person writing to one reader
- [ ] One idea per paragraph; visible gaps; no wall of text
- [ ] One concrete detail + one admitted feeling; belief stated plainly
- [ ] Product enters as resolution; brand named sparingly
- [ ] Signature/P.S. land when used (often founder/CEO/status); unsigned still feels singular
- [ ] Within length caps; every claim validated; no absolutes/banned phrases
- [ ] Read-aloud test passes
