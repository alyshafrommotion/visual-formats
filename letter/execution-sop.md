# Letter Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

- **Brain location:** `/agent/brain/2. ideation/visual-formats/letter/`
- This SOP lives in that folder, so the example creatives sit next to it.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, open that folder and study the examples first** — letter length, the salutation/sign-off treatment, and how personal vs promotional they read.

## What this format is

A block of earnest, first-person written copy set to look like a letter or personal note: a salutation, body paragraphs that argue or confess, a signature. It works because a letter signals sincerity and singular address — one person writing to one reader — and because long copy that earns attention converts a considered buyer better than a slogan.

Strongest for **mission/values, apology or trust-repair, price/quality justification, a big change or relaunch, and warm/retargeting audiences** who will read. Weak for pure impulse promo.

The one property to protect: **it must read like a person wrote it to one reader,** not like a brand broadcasting. The first line and the sign-off do most of the work.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the argument or admission), persona and awareness stage, the real **brand/founder context or customer story**, and claim facts. The angle sets the point; the brand context supplies the truth and the voice; this SOP governs structure and register.

If there's no genuine reason for a letter (a real belief, change, apology, or story), don't force it — a letter with nothing to say reads as costume.

## Anatomy — the layers

### 1 · Salutation
- Direct, human, singular. "Hey," "To the person who's tried everything," "Dear over-thinker." Names the reader or the moment, not the segment.

### 2 · The opening line (the hook)
- The first sentence is the scroll-stopper: a confession, a sharp belief, a problem named plainly, or a promise. It must make the reader want the next line. No throat-clearing.

### 3 · The body (2–5 short paragraphs)
- One idea per paragraph, on a clear spine: **problem/belief → what we did → what it means for you.**
- Short paragraphs with visible gaps — the whitespace paces the read. Never one wall.
- Product enters as the resolution, not the subject.

### 4 · The sign-off
- A real name (founder/team), a role, sometimes a P.S. that carries the offer or the single strongest line. Handwritten-signature styling optional.

## Register rules (priority order)

1. **Write to one person.** Second-person singular, warm, direct. "You," not "our customers."
2. **Open on the hook, not the greeting's throat-clear.** The first body line earns the rest.
3. **One idea per paragraph; short paragraphs.** Gaps do the pacing.
4. **Story and belief beat features.** Lead with the lived thing or the conviction; product is the resolution.
5. **One mundane concrete detail and one admitted feeling.** Specificity + humanity are what make a letter land.
6. **Say the belief plainly.** A letter is allowed conviction and vulnerability a banner isn't. Use them.
7. **Specificity is credibility.** Real numbers, real decisions. No absolutes.
8. **Brand named sparingly, mid-letter,** the way a person refers to their own thing. The P.S. may carry one clear ask.
9. **Read aloud.** A letter has to sound like a voice. Rewrite anything you stumble on. Cut every sentence that doesn't advance the argument or the feeling.
10. **Banned:** "we're excited to announce," "we're on a mission to," corporate connectors (moreover/furthermore), "designed to," "seamless," "solutions," and any paragraph doing two jobs.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Salutation | ≤6 words |
| Opening line | 1 sentence, ≤18 words |
| Body | 2–5 paragraphs, each 1–3 sentences, sentences ≤18 words |
| Whole letter | consumer 70–140 words · considered/high-AOV up to ~200 |
| P.S. | 1 sentence, carries the ask or the sharpest line |

Cut order when over: the summary paragraph first (the letter already ended), then qualifiers and second adjectives. Never fix length by densifying — dense reads corporate; short reads human.

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage; confirm there's a real reason for a letter.
2. Pull the true story/belief and the one concrete detail from brand context.
3. Choose the spine (problem → what we did → what it means).
4. Write the opening line first; make it the scroll-stopper.
5. Draft the body, one idea per paragraph; product as resolution.
6. Write the sign-off and a P.S. that carries the ask or best line.
7. Read-aloud + register pass (rules 1–10).
8. Length pass and claims pass (validate every number).
9. Design handoff: letter/note styling, generous paragraph gaps, signature treatment, quiet or no product imagery so the words lead.
10. Deliver: salutation, opening line, body paragraphs in order, sign-off, P.S., styling note, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Reads like one person writing to one reader
- [ ] Opening line is a real hook, not a greeting throat-clear
- [ ] One idea per paragraph; visible gaps; no wall of text
- [ ] One concrete detail + one admitted feeling; belief stated plainly
- [ ] Product enters as resolution; brand named sparingly
- [ ] Within length caps; every claim validated; no absolutes/banned phrases
- [ ] Read-aloud test passes; sign-off + P.S. land
