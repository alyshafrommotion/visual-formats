# Letter — Classification Guide

## Definition

A block of **earnest, first-person written copy** styled to look like a real letter or personal note: a salutation, body paragraphs that argue or confess, and a sign-off (name/role, optional P.S.). Written copy carries the whole ad, no designed banner treatment.

## The two things that must both be true

1. **It has letter structure**: a salutation/opening address, body paragraphs, and a sign-off. It reads as one person writing to one reader.
2. **The visual treatment looks like a letter or note**, not a designed ad banner, headline-and-CTA layout, or native app UI mimicry.

## Quick test

Ask: *if I printed this out, would it look like a letter someone wrote?* If yes, Letter. If it's a headline/CTA banner, a native-app text treatment, or a spoken video, it's a different format.

## Commonly confused with

- **Founder:** carries the same first-person origin/belief content, but Founder is spoken on camera; Letter is written copy styled as a letter. If it's read, not watched, it's `letter`.
- **Post-it:** also handwritten-feeling, but Post-it is a short single-thought sticky note (≤12 words), not multi-paragraph letter structure with a salutation and sign-off.
- **Instagram Text Overlay:** native app-styled text treatment, not letter structure (no salutation/sign-off).

## What to look for as supporting signals

- Second-person address ("you," not "our customers")
- Product enters as the resolution, near the end
- A P.S. that carries the offer or the sharpest line
