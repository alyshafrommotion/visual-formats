# Founder — Classification Guide

## Definition

A **video** in which the **person who actually started the company or built the product** speaks on camera, talking about the product as the person who created it: why it exists, how it was developed, and the mission behind it. First-person, authority-driven, a peek behind the curtain only the founder can give. Founder ads are always videos, never statics.

## The things that must all be true

1. **It's a video.** The founder is speaking on camera. A static carrying founder copy is not this format (that's closer to `letter`).
2. **The speaker is the actual founder/builder** (or is clearly presented as such), not a hired creator, a customer, or a non-founder employee.
3. **They speak as the person who created it.** They talk on behalf of the brand as its originator, usually getting into product development, the origin problem, or the mission, material only the founder would have.

If any one of those is missing, it's a different format.

## Quick test

Ask: *is this a video of the person who started the company or built the product, talking as the originator about why it exists or how it was made?* If yes, Founder. If the speaker represents the brand but didn't create it, it's EGC. If it's a non-founder fast talking-head, it's `yapper`. If the origin/mission is delivered as written copy, it's `letter`.

## How you can tell it's the founder (classification signals)

- **Origination language:** "we made this," "I created this," "we started this company," "I couldn't find X so I built it," "when we were developing this." A creator or employee does not claim to have created or started the company.
- **In-the-weeds product knowledge:** they talk about developing the product, the formulation/design decisions, the trade-offs they chose, the mission from day one. Detail only the originator would have.
- **A peek behind the curtain:** something viewers couldn't get without hearing it from the founder, which earns them a relationship to the brand.

## Commonly confused with

- **EGC (employee-generated content):** an employee of the brand speaks on behalf of the brand, so it can sound similar, but they are not the founder. Employees talk more high-level and less in the weeds; they don't claim to have created or started the company and can't give founder-level product-development or origin detail. If the speaker represents the brand but isn't the originator, it's EGC, not Founder. (EGC does not have its own folder in this library yet.)
- **Yapper:** also a talking-head monologue, but the speaker isn't the founder and the content is a fast persuasive rant/argument rather than an origin/development/mission story. No founder identity and no origin spine means `yapper`.
- **Letter:** carries the same origin/belief/mission content, but delivered as written copy styled like a letter, not spoken on camera. If it's read rather than a video of the founder speaking, it's `letter`.

## What to look for as supporting signals

- One concrete lived detail plus one admitted feeling
- Product enters as the resolution of the story, not the subject of the video
- Setting is not a signal either way: a real, non-studio space is common but not required. A studio or polished setup is fine. The authority comes from who is speaking and what only they can say, not the production level.
