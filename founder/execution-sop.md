# Founder Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

- **Reference examples:** They sit in this same folder, right next to this SOP.
- This SOP lives in this folder, so the example creatives (videos) sit next to it.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, open this folder and watch the examples first** — how the founder opens, how much is story vs product, what they reveal about building the product, and the level of polish.

## What this format is

The founder, on camera, telling you why the product exists and how it was made, in words only they can credibly say. It is **always a video**. It leans on authority and motive over production polish, but a studio or polished setup is completely fine: the credibility comes from who is speaking and what only they can reveal, not the setting. It works because a founder can make claims, admissions, and behind-the-scenes reveals a paid creator or a non-founder employee can't, and because motive reads as honesty.

The founder edge is **access**: product development, the origin problem, the decisions and trade-offs, the mission from day one. Give viewers a peek behind the curtain they could only get from the person who built it, and it earns them a relationship to the brand they couldn't have otherwise. (An employee speaking for the brand, more high-level and less in the weeds, is EGC, not Founder — see `classification.md`.)

Strongest for **origin/mission angles, category reframes, trust-repair, price/quality justification, and "why we made this differently."** Weak for pure promo or discount-led asks (use a different format for those).

The one property to protect: **it has to sound like the founder talking, not the brand talking through them.** First person, specific, a little imperfect.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the case to make), persona and awareness stage, the real **founder story / brand context** (why it was built, how it was developed, what they rejected, what they believe), and any claim facts. The angle picks the argument; the brand context supplies the true story; this SOP governs structure and script voice.

If the real founder story isn't available, gather it — this format cannot be faked from features. A generic founder script is just an ad with a face on it.

## Anatomy — the beats

Pick ONE spine; don't stack all of them.

- **Origin problem → what I built** — "I kept running into X, no one fixed it, so we did." Default for cold audiences.
- **Belief/manifesto** — "Most of this category does X. We refuse to." For category reframes.
- **Concession → conviction** — "This costs more, and here's why I won't change it." For price/quality.
- **Behind-the-decision / product-development peek** — how it was actually made, or one specific choice they made and the cost of it: development detail only the originator would know. For trust, differentiation, and giving viewers a relationship to the brand they couldn't get otherwise.

### The 4 script sections
1. **Hook (0–3s):** the founder says the one line that stops the scroll — a confession, a strong belief, or the problem stated plainly. Never "Hi, I'm [name], founder of…" as the opener; identity comes after the hook earns it.
2. **The story/stakes:** past tense, lived, specific. One concrete detail that proves it happened. This is where founder authority lives.
3. **The turn:** what they did about it / what makes the product the answer. Product enters here, as the resolution of the story, not a feature dump.
4. **The ask:** plain, warm, singular. One next step, in the founder's voice.

## Script / voice rules (priority order)

1. **First person, as the originator, always.** "I made this," "we started this," "I couldn't." The moment it slips to "our customers love," it's an ad again — and the moment it could be said by someone who didn't build the company, it's EGC, not Founder.
2. **Open on the hook, not the intro.** Earn the name-drop by the second or third line.
3. **One mundane concrete detail and one admitted feeling.** The detail makes it true; the feeling makes it a person.
4. **Story beats claims.** Lead with the lived thing; let the product be the resolution, not the subject.
5. **Say the belief out loud, and reveal something only the founder knows.** Founders are allowed conviction a creator isn't, and access to product-development detail an employee doesn't have. Use both.
6. **Specificity is credibility.** Real numbers, real decisions, real trade-offs. No absolutes; skeptics screenshot them.
7. **Product named the way a person says it,** once or twice, mid-story. Never a positioning phrase attached to the name.
8. **Dictate before scripting.** Say it out loud as if explaining to a friend, transcribe, trim. Read every line aloud; rewrite anything you stumble on.
9. **Banned:** "Hi I'm [name] and I'm the founder of…" as the hook, "we're on a mission to," "game-changer," "designed to," "seamless," and any line that could be read by anyone but this founder.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Hook | 1 line, ≤12 words, spoken in the first 3s |
| Total runtime | 20–45s (up to 60s for a considered/high-AOV case) |
| Sentences | short, spoken-length; ≤15 words |
| Product mentions | 1–2, mid-story |
| The ask | 1 sentence, 1 action |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage.
2. Pull the real founder story and belief from brand context; mark the one detail that proves it and the one product-development reveal only the founder could give.
3. Choose one spine.
4. Write the hook line first — the scroll-stopper the founder can uniquely say.
5. Script the story → turn → ask; keep product as the resolution.
6. Dictate-transcribe-trim; read aloud pass.
7. Register + claims pass (rules 1–9; validate every number).
8. **Production handoff:** eye-line to camera, minimal cuts, captions on. Setting is flexible: their real space or a studio both work, so pick whatever serves the story. Note b-roll only where it supports the story.
9. Deliver: hook line, full VO script by section, setting/casting note, on-screen text, claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] It's a video of the founder speaking (never a static)
- [ ] The founder reads as the actual originator (origination language, or product-development/mission detail only they could give — not an employee speaking for the brand)
- [ ] Hook lands in the first 3s and is something only the founder could say
- [ ] First person throughout; name-drop comes after the hook
- [ ] One concrete detail + one admitted feeling
- [ ] Product enters as the story's resolution, not a feature dump
- [ ] One clear ask in the founder's voice
- [ ] Every claim validated; no absolutes; no banned lines
- [ ] Read-aloud test passes on every line
