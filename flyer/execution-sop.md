# Flyer — Execution SOP

## When to use

- The tone calls for **grassroots, scrappy, pattern-interrupt** energy: challenger and underdog brands, community or local angles, playful gags.
- Strong fits: missing/wanted-poster jokes, lost-pet notices, band-flyer tear-tabs, apartment-for-rent notices, community-board announcements.
- **Funnel fit:** TOF–MOF. The handmade, non-ad look lowers ad recognition and buys a beat of curiosity, then the sheet delivers the pitch.

## Build

1. **Pick the flyer trope that carries the message.** The recognizable real-world flyer format (wanted, missing, lost, free, for-rent, open-mic) is the vessel; bend it to the product point.
2. **Keep it to ONE printed sheet.** Handmade cues sell it: limited or mono ink, a slight skew, real paper texture, a headline set big like a poster.
3. **Stage the placement in a believable spot.** Pole, community board, elevator panel, laundromat, storefront glass. Add tape/staples and, when it helps, a hand or passerby for scale and realism.
4. **Message-within-the-message.** Headline gag up top, product(s) shown small as the "item/suspect/reward," and the offer as the payoff line (e.g. "REWARD: 20% off").
5. **CTA stays in the flyer's world when possible** — a tear-off tab, a QR code, a "scan me," or a scrawled URL. It keeps the illusion intact.

## Pairs well with

- **meme** (the trope gag itself), **listicle** or **product-grid** (when the sheet shows a lineup), **review** (a testimonial styled as a posted note). Tag the inner treatment as the secondary format.

## Watch-outs

- **Don't make it glossy or polished.** Clean, paid-looking production pulls it toward `ad-in-the-wild` and kills the grassroots effect that makes flyer work.
- **Legibility.** Flyers get busy fast. Hold to one clear headline and one offer; let the placement do the interrupting.
- **Believability of the spot.** A flyer taped somewhere no one tapes flyers breaks the illusion. Keep the placement true to where these actually appear.
