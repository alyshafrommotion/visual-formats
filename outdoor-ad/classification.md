# Outdoor Ad (OOH) — Classification Guide

## Definition

An ad staged to look like a **physical out-of-home placement** — a billboard, building wall, bus shelter, or transit poster — composited into a **real-world street or public scene**, usually with passersby, sidewalk, and city backdrop. The message-within-the-message is the brand's creative; the outer frame makes it look like a big, real-world ad someone is walking past. Usually a static image.

## The things that must all be true

1. **The brand's message is shown as an out-of-home surface** (billboard, wall, poster, transit) rather than filling the whole frame as a plain static.
2. **It's set in a real-world outdoor/public scene** — street, sidewalk, building, sky — often with a person or people passing by for scale and realism.
3. **The OOH placement is the device** — the "wow, that's a real billboard" framing is the point, not just a background texture.

## Quick test

Does it look like a photo of the brand's ad living on a real billboard/wall/poster in a public space? If yes, Outdoor Ad. If the message just fills the frame with no OOH surface, it's a different format.

## Commonly confused with

- **The *inner* creative's own format:** the poster on the billboard can itself be another format (a letter, a feature-benefit-pointout, etc.). Outdoor Ad describes the *outer* OOH staging; tag the inner treatment as a secondary format when it's useful.
- **Press:** borrows third-party editorial credibility (publication logos, pull-quotes). Outdoor Ad borrows the scale and legitimacy of a real-world media placement, not a publication.
- **Lifestyle/product static:** a plain product-in-a-scene with no simulated ad surface is not an Outdoor Ad.

## What to look for as supporting signals

- Billboard, wall-scape, bus shelter, or transit frame around the creative
- Street furniture, sidewalk, sky, buildings; a pedestrian mid-stride for scale
- Often AI-composited or mocked-up rather than a genuine photographed placement
