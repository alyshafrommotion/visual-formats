# Press Format — Implementation SOP

## Reference examples (look here first)

- **Brain location:** `/agent/brain/2. ideation/visual-formats/press/`
- This SOP lives in that folder, so the example creatives sit next to it.

**Whenever this SOP is triggered or referenced, open that folder and study the examples first** — how logos and pull-quotes are arranged, how many, and how the product sits inside the editorial frame.

## What this format is

An ad that borrows the visual language and authority of the press: publication logos, a pull-quote styled like a magazine headline, "as seen in," awards, or a mocked-up editorial layout. It works because third-party authority converts skepticism into trust — someone credible vouched, so the buyer doesn't have to take the brand's word.

Strongest for **credibility building, category entry, higher-consideration or higher-priced products, and cold/skeptical audiences** who need external proof. Requires **real coverage, awards, or quotes** to use ethically and effectively.

The one property to protect: **every quote and logo must be real and attributable.** Fabricated or vague press is a legal and trust liability; specificity is the whole point.

## Inputs

Expect a **messaging angle** (the claim the press proves), persona and awareness stage, the real **press assets** (publication names, exact quotes with attribution, dates, awards, ratings), and claim facts. The angle picks the proof to lead with; the press assets supply it; this SOP governs arrangement and copy.

If there's no real press, don't fake it — use a testimonial/social-proof format instead. This format is only as strong as the coverage behind it.

## Anatomy — the layers

### 1 · The pull-quote (hero)
- One editorial-styled headline quote, set large like a magazine cover line. The single strongest, most specific line from real coverage.
- Attributed clearly: publication (and writer/outlet where it strengthens it). A quote with no source is worthless here.

### 2 · The logo bar / "as seen in"
- A tidy row of real publication logos, or awards/ratings. 3–6, recognizable to the persona. Quality over quantity.

### 3 · The product + editorial frame
- Product shot inside a layout that reads editorial: serif type, clean grid, magazine-like spacing. The design does the "credible" signaling; keep it restrained.

### 4 · The claim/benefit line + CTA
- One line connecting the press to what it means for the buyer, then a quiet CTA. The press proves it; this line translates it.

## Craft rules (priority order)

1. **Real and attributable, always.** Every quote sourced, every logo earned. No paraphrase presented as a quote.
2. **Lead with the most specific quote,** not the most flattering adjective. "Cut my CPA in half in 3 weeks" beats "amazing product."
3. **Editorial restraint.** Serif, grid, whitespace — let the layout signal credibility. Overdesign breaks the illusion.
4. **Recognizable proof for this persona.** Three logos they trust beat ten they don't.
5. **Translate the press into a benefit** in one line — don't assume the quote self-explains.
6. **Specificity is credibility.** Real numbers, real outlets, real dates. No absolutes.
7. **Brand present but secondary to the proof.** The press is the star; the brand is what it points to.
8. **Banned:** unattributed quotes, invented outlets, "critically acclaimed"/"award-winning" with no named award, "designed to," "seamless," and any adjective standing in for a real quote.

## Length caps

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| Pull-quote | 1 line, ≤14 words, attributed |
| Logo/award bar | 3–6 items |
| Benefit line | ≤12 words |
| CTA | ≤6 words |

## Build steps

1. Read the brief: angle, persona, awareness stage.
2. Gather real press assets; verify every quote, attribution, logo, and award.
3. Pick the single strongest, most specific quote as the hero.
4. Choose 3–6 recognizable logos/awards for the persona.
5. Write the benefit line that translates the press into what the buyer gets.
6. Claims pass: every quote/logo/award/number validated and sourced.
7. Design handoff: editorial/serif layout, restrained grid, product inside the frame, attribution legible.
8. Deliver: hero pull-quote + attribution, logo/award list, benefit line, CTA, layout note, sources/claims flags.

## QA checklist
- [ ] Every quote and logo is real and attributable
- [ ] Hero quote is the most specific, not the most flattering
- [ ] 3–6 recognizable proof items; layout reads editorial and restrained
- [ ] One benefit line translates the press into buyer value
- [ ] Every claim/number sourced; no absolutes; no invented outlets/awards
- [ ] Attribution legible at thumb size
